## Why QP4?

QP1 found finite-chain parity from a spin-1 Heisenberg Hamiltonian. QP2 computed site spin and string correlations. QP3 matched the same finite observables across the single-ion anisotropy D. These observables do not, alone, identify a thermodynamic Haldane phase.

Known literature emphasizes spectral gaps and finite-size scaling in the Haldane/large-D setting. See Veríssimo et al., *Physical Review B* **104**, 024409 (2021), DOI:10.1103/PhysRevB.104.024409; and Golinelli, Jolicoeur & Lacaze, *Physical Review B* **45**, 9798 (1992), DOI:10.1103/PhysRevB.45.9798. **QP4 does not reproduce those published methods or their quantitative critical estimates.**

## Frozen comparison

Use the QP1 spin-1 exchange Hamiltonian and QP3 on-site anisotropy:

```text
H = +1 * sum_<i,j> S_i · S_j + D * sum_i (Sz_i)^2
```

- **N = 4, 6, 8**.
- **D/J = 0, 1, 4, 12**, no interpolation or claims of continuity.
- **Open chains and periodic rings**, with identical parameters otherwise.
- All nonnegative magnetization **M = 0,1,...,N** sectors are diagonalized for the *lowest energy* of each. Negative M states have the same spectrum from spin-reversal symmetry of this Hamiltonian.
- In the M=0 sector, also calculate the *second* lowest eigenenergy.
- Ground energy must be in M=0 and strictly below the lowest states in other sectors within fixed numerical tolerance (for these **even N** only).
- Report the **first positive finite-energy excitation** found between M0's second level and the minimum positive gap across M>0 sectors. Also disclose the **neutral M=0 gap** and **magnetization-changing gap** separately, with the first-excitation sector label.
- These gaps are **finite many-body spectrum differences** in units of J, not tunneling peaks, a bulk Haldane gap, or a laboratory spectrum.
- Dense Hermitian diagonalization for small sectors; deterministic-start sparse ARPACK `eigsh` for larger sectors.
- QP4 intentionally reuses the QP1 matrix construction and QP3's D grid. The D=0 excitations must agree with QP1 for both boundaries; the ground energy of all QP4 open rows must agree with QP3.

## Acceptance gates

1. Exactly 24 rows = 3 N × 4 D × 2 boundary conditions; all finite gaps strictly positive.
2. M=0 lowest level is the global finite-system ground state for the sampled even N and D values.
3. For D=0, computed excitation gap and energy match QP1's existing reference within 1e-7 for both geometries.
4. For every open-chain row, ground M=0 energy matches QP3 within 1e-7.
5. Check every M=0..N sector minimum; no extrapolated gap inferred from a missing magnetization sector.
6. Publish *separate* neutral and magnetization-changing gaps alongside the lower gap. No claim that a minimum on this coarse D grid is a transition.
7. Recompute the JSON with the Python module in GitHub Actions for both standard reproducibility CI and before the React build. Browser never diagonalizes a quantum Hamiltonian.
8. Metadata must stay `SIMULATED / authority NONE / attested false`; no updates to frozen QP1/2/3 artifacts.

## Scientific boundaries and next experiment

N=4,6,8 and four D values are not enough for a defensible finite-size scaling fit, any critical-point estimate, or a symmetry-protected-topological classification. A finite spectral gap, by itself, cannot distinguish Haldane from large-D gapped regimes. More precise work would need e.g. larger N, boundary twists/entanglement or validated continuum extrapolation, predeclared error model and independent benchmarks. This experiment supplies finite evidence and controls, not a claim of new particle physics.
